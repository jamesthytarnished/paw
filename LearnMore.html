You've Got This: A Note for First-Time Dog and Cat Owners

Updated: 7/30/2026

If you're a first-time dog owner, first-time cat owner, or a seasoned pet parent, congratulations on expanding your family with a new four-legged member!

Bringing home a new dog or cat is a time-honored experience, and one that is fulfilling for both pet and pet parent. The next chapter of your lives together is beginning!

It can be frustrating, overwhelming, joyful, and exciting all at the same time. But don't worry. With preparation, patience, and love, you and your new companion can build a wonderful relationship.

Pet-Proof Your Home

Bringing home your new companion is an exciting step in the process, and one that takes a little bit of preparation to make the transition as smooth as possible. To get ready for your new dog or cat, you will need to think about pet-proofing your home. Consider adding temporary baby gates to your home to keep them from entering certain areas and/or keeping them away from other dogs, cats, and children until properly introduced. Dogs and cats can be surprisingly smart and can open cabinets and drawers. Restrict reachable areas like these with childproof latches to prevent them from getting into things they shouldn’t. Medicine and cleaning supplies should be put in an unreachable place or locked up. Animals are curious, and trash cans can be ground zero for their exploration, so consider getting one with a latch or one that is hard to open. Some pets might see an electrical cord and think it’s a chew toy, so it’s best to secure them with chew-proof protectors, especially if you have a puppy or kitten who is extra curious as they explore the new world around them. Some houseplants can be poisonous to pets, such as lilies. It’s best to remove them from your home or place them out of reach. Cats are known as playful and curious beings for a reason, so be sure to anchor large pieces of furniture like bookshelves and armoires to prevent them from tipping over, and come up with a system to make sure that they don’t have access to chemically treated toilet bowl water.

Budget Basics for New Pet Parents

How much your new pet will cost you monthly or annually depends on the dog or cat’s stage of life, size, your lifestyle, and other factors. For example, a puppy or senior dog could go to the vet more than an adult one. Larger breeds will need to eat more food, and dogs with high-maintenance grooming breeds like Havanese and Bichons will cost more per year to maintain their coats.

You might prefer a self-cleaning, state-of-the-art litter box over scooping kitty litter, or your cat breed might have high enrichment needs such as Bengal and Siamese. There are other factors like pet sitters, dog walkers, specialty harnesses, cat trees, and more to think about as well.

You’re Ready to Welcome Your New Pet Home

Now that you know the essentials for bringing home your new pet, it’s time to get started. Go slow, follow their lead, and give both you and your new pet grace as you figure each other out during the first three months. Paying attention to the details now is how you become bonded for life.
